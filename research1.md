## Current research

### aWall — Agile and Hybrid Collaboration (Ongoing)

aWall is an ongoing research initiative at our institute investigating how **large interactive digital card walls** can support planning, coordination and collaboration in agile software development teams. A particular focus is **hybrid collaboration**: enabling people working on-site and remotely to share an effective visual workspace.

The research builds on earlier aWall projects in interactive card walls, multi-project coordination and online collaboration. Current work investigates **information asymmetry in hybrid agile teams**, particularly how differences in access to project updates affect situational awareness and coordination. It explores visual awareness mechanisms, ways to reduce change blindness, and AI-supported approaches to finding and sharing relevant information.

Recent supervised student projects contribute to this direction through information visualization, mechanisms for identifying unseen changes, and AI-assisted awareness during hybrid meetings. [Explore supervised student research](/student-research).

### AI Integrated Hybrid Meetings (2023–2024)

This research project investigated the integration of **AI-supported analysis of transcribed video and audio meetings and chat messages** into hybrid collaboration workflows.

The aim was to explore how relevant information arising in discussions could be identified and brought into a shared visual workspace, helping agile teams maintain common awareness, coordinate their work and reduce the manual effort of keeping card walls up to date.

This project forms part of the ongoing aWall research direction, rather than a replacement for the broader work on interactive card walls.

## Previous projects

The following project descriptions document earlier research activities. The aWall projects below are completed project phases within the continuing aWall research initiative.

### aWall — Agile Multi-Project Coordination (2020–2021)

This project investigated how the aWall approach and large interactive displays could support coordination across multiple agile projects. The focus was on transparency, shared overviews and lightweight coordination at organisational scale.

### aWall — Agile Collaboration Wall (2018–2019)

Building on earlier research, this project developed aWall further as a collaboration tool for agile teams using large multi-touch displays. It explored online collaboration for distributed teams, a “digital scrum master” concept, and multimodal interaction methods. The work also contributed to the development of [aTouch Dynamics](https://www.atouch.ch/).

  Partners: FHNW, aTouch Dynamics
  Funding: Aargauer Forschungsfonds

### 4th Swiss Agile Study (2018–2019)

The fourth Swiss Agile Study expanded the research through international cooperation with the Agile Research Network in the UK and Victoria University of Wellington in New Zealand. Related surveys offered opportunities to compare agile working practices across countries.

  Partners: FHNW, ZHAW, SwissICT, SWEN, ARN (UK), Victoria University of Wellington (NZ)
  Funding: Hasler Stiftung Switzerland, SwissICT, SWEN
  Web: [www.swissagilestudy.ch](http://www.swissagilestudy.ch/).

### 3rd Swiss Agile Study (2016–2017)

The third Swiss Agile Study investigated the use of software-development methodologies and practices in Swiss IT organisations. It provided further empirical insights into agile working practices and their organisational context.

  Partners: ZHAW, SwissICT, SWEN
  Funding: Hasler Stiftung Switzerland, SwissICT, SWEN
  Web: [www.swissagilestudy.ch](http://www.swissagilestudy.ch/).

### 2nd Swiss Agile Study (2014–2015)

The second nationwide study examined the adoption of agile software-development methodologies in Swiss companies and among IT professionals, including questions of organisational culture.

  Partners: ZHAW, SwissICT, ICTnet, SWEN
  Funding: Hasler Stiftung Switzerland, SwissICT, ICTnet, SWEN
  Web: [www.swissagilestudy.ch](http://www.swissagilestudy.ch/).

### Agile Success Factors (2013–2015)

Building on the 2012 Swiss Agile Study, this research project investigated factors contributing to successful agile software projects. In collaboration with the university of applied sciences in Winterthur, it examined experiences from companies and sought patterns across different project and organisation types.

  Partners: University of Applied Sciences Zurich Winterthur
  Funding: Hasler Foundation, Switzerland.

### Agile Technology for Agile Methods (SI-ATAM, 2013–2014)

This interdisciplinary project investigated how ultra-large multi-touch displays could support collaboration in agile software development. It explored teamwork in collaborative environments and developed interaction concepts for wall-sized touch systems.

  Partners: School of Business (FHNW), School of Applied Psychology
  Funding: Strategy Fund of UAS Northwestern Switzerland
  Web: [SI-ATAM](http://www.fhnw.ch/technik/imvs/forschung/projekte/si-atam/si-atam)

### 1st Swiss Agile Study (2012–2013)

The first nationwide Swiss Agile Study examined software-development methodologies and practices among Swiss IT companies and professionals. Further information and the study report were published on [www.swissagilestudy.ch](http://www.swissagilestudy.ch/).

  Partners: ZHAW, SwissICT, ICTnet, SWEN
  Funding: Hasler Stiftung Switzerland, SwissICT, ICTnet, SWEN
  Web: [www.swissagilestudy.ch](http://www.swissagilestudy.ch/).

### ReFit (2010–2012)

ReFit was an Eclipse plug-in for the automated refactoring of Fit acceptance tests, originating from the ProMedServices project. The plug-in was published as an open-source project on [SourceForge](http://sourceforge.net/projects/fitrefactoring/).

  Funding: Hasler Stiftung Switzerland, Förderverein Fachhochschule Nordwestschweiz

### ProMedServices — ProActive Software Service Evolution (2007–2009)

This project investigated techniques for analysing and evolving software services in a large distributed system. Drawing on architecture reconstruction, refactoring, service engineering and testing, it explored how to detect software decay and support proactive improvement of software quality.

  Partners: University Hospital Geneva (HUG); University of Zurich, ifi-SEAL
  Funding: Hasler Stiftung Switzerland

### BEAP — Beamline Experimental Application (2008)

Developed a high-speed TCP/IP and UDP communication library in .NET and C# for accessing and controlling an accelerator beamline using the EPICS protocol.

  Funding: HaPaul-Scherrer-Institute (PSI)

### CIDB — Controls Inventory Database (2005–2007)

Developed a web-based system for managing and configuring hardware components in the Paul Scherrer Institute's accelerator systems.

  Paul-Scherrer-Institute (PSI)

### Module Testing in Practice (2001–2003)

This project developed and applied a unit-testing environment for a distributed real-time control-system framework, addressing the challenges of testing software with real-time requirements and distributed components.

  Ammann Aufbereitung, Langenthal; HTA Luzern; soft[net]

### WED — Web Enabled Devices (2001–2003)

As part of the Edison competence network, this joint project with institutions in Sion and Lucerne investigated small web-enabled embedded systems and the use of standard web-services protocols to integrate them into IT infrastructure.

  HEV Sion; HTA Luzern; soft[net]

### Foundation of SWEN — Software Engineering Network (2000–2003)

The institutions in Lucerne, St. Gallen and Northwestern Switzerland established a competence network for software engineering under the Swiss Soft[net] programme. The network promoted technology transfer, cooperation and the exchange of expertise between universities and industry.

  FHO St. Gallen, HTA Luzern; soft[net]

### SWEED — Software Engineering Education (2000–2002)

This collaborative project developed a framework curriculum and teaching modules for software-engineering education at Swiss universities of applied sciences, in cooperation with universities and industry partners. I contributed to the curriculum and developed a three-day course, “Introduction into UML”, as well as work on an Object-Oriented Analysis module.

  FHO St. Gallen, HTA Luzern; soft[net]
